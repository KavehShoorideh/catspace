The Opponent's Veto — visualization options

Seven ways to show one claim, each with a ▶ demo that drives itself. Boards are lichess's own renderer (chessground). EXACT-TB = real tablebase ground truth; MOCK = interaction prototype; LEARNED-PENDING = upgrades when the trained field passes its gate.

Two reachabilities live on the same board: what could happen, and what your opponent will allow. The gap between them is the opponent, as geometry. A mistake shrinks the gap — and a tactic is the forcing line that cashes the lapse before it closes. Each tab is one way to see this. Every tab has a ▶ demo button: press it and the page will work the controls itself while narrating what to notice.

Measured on the K+2R vs K+B+P endgame laboratory: n=1200 targets from 20 won positions, 4-ply horizon, tablebase-exact (VERDICT ADVERSARIAL_VETO, 2026-07-23).

A — the two cones EXACT-TB

What you're looking at: your next 4 plies as a cone of 300 winning futures fanning out from “now” (the black dot). How to read it: every dot is a position you could reach if the opponent cooperated; green dots you can force; rust dots he simply steers away from. The proportions are the measured 13% / 87%.
  • Try: press ▶ demo — the cone re-deals and the denied mass fades out, leaving the thread you can actually plan along.
Press ▶ demo.

B — the opportunity seismograph EXACT-TB

What you're looking at: a real game — engine attacker vs tablebase-optimal defender, with two random (blunder) moves injected. Before White's every move we ask, for a fixed panel of 30 winning regions: how many can be forced right now? That fraction is the amber line. How to read it: dashed red verticals = the injected blunders; the line moving after them = opportunity appearing/consumed. The board replays the game — the last move is highlighted, lichess-style.
  • Try: ◀ ▶ arrows (keyboard works), click any point on the line, or press ▶ demo to watch the game play itself with narration.
Press ▶ demo, or step through the game.

Prototype caveat: the exact-DFS panel is material-bound, so conversion also closes regions; the learned field will track a chosen basin at any distance. LEARNED-PENDING

C — the confinement heatmap EXACT-TB

What you're looking at: the moment right before a real blunder. Circles mark where the defender's king could end up in the sampled winning futures: green = you can force his king there within 4 plies, red = only reachable if he helps you. How to read it: toggle before/after his blunder — squares flip green. That flip IS a tactical opportunity appearing.
  • Try: the before/after buttons, or ▶ demo to watch the flip with a count.

The blunder opens new forceable king-destinations — Kaveh's “opportunity suddenly made itself available,” painted on the squares where every chess player already looks.

D — the watchlist feed EXACT-TB

What you're looking at: the engine's “spidey sense” as a notification stream. Between White's moves, the same 30-region panel is re-checked; every event says which opportunities opened or closed and after whose move. How to read it: green-edged = opportunities opened; rust-edged = closed (consumed or escaped).
  • Try: click any event — the board shows that moment with the opened king-destinations circled. ▶ demo walks the feed top to bottom.
Click an event or press ▶ demo.

E — the opponent dial MOCK

What you're looking at: the same position, evaluated against opponents of different strengths. How to read it: as the slider drops, denied regions open (weaker defense = weaker veto), and trap regions appear: objectively defensible, but this opponent won't find the defense.
  • Try: drag the slider, or ▶ demo sweeps it for you. Interaction is mocked; the real version reads the graded-defender channel + the opponent model's seeing-probability.
